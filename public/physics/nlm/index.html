R1
Zero net force allows steady motion
MIC-PHY-NLM-NET-ZERO-MOTION · CAP-NLM-NET-ZERO-MOTION
Default Lane MEDIUM Pos: 20
Learner Capability Assessment: CAP-NLM-NET-ZERO-MOTION
Error Stage: Governed Repair: NLM1-1
Primary Capability CAP-NLM-NET-ZERO-MOTION
Capability Action Relate zero net force to unchanged velocity.
Prerequisites None
Intrinsic Rationale Everyday friction makes continued motion look as if it needs a continuing push.
🎯 What It Teaches (Inferential Jump)
Zero net force does not mean zero motion; it means velocity does not change, so rest and steady motion are both compatible with the same condition.
⚖️ Controlled Variation (Rung Must Contain)
MUST CONTAIN:
  • A case that keeps moving after the push has ended.
  • A case at rest with no change in motion, treated by the same rule.
CONTROLLED PHASES:
Phase 1: Vary whether a brief push is still in contact while holding the cart's direction and an approximately frictionless surface after release.
→ Notice: the cart can continue moving after the applied push becomes zero
Phase 2: Vary the initial velocity from zero to nonzero while holding zero net horizontal force.
→ Notice: both rest and steady motion fit the same zero-change condition
⚠️ Common Misconception & Repair
Wrong Idea: "A force must keep acting in the direction of motion or the object must stop."
Diagnostic: A low-friction cart is pushed and then released. After the hand loses contact, what horizontal force is required merely to keep it moving steadily?
Repair: Separate velocity from change of velocity: a net force is required for acceleration, not for the existence of motion.
🏁 Exit Task & Success Criterion
One cart is at rest and another moves steadily in a straight line. For each, decide whether zero net force is compatible with the motion and explain the common rule.
Model Response: Zero net force is compatible with both. Each cart can keep its current velocity unchanged.
Check: Ask whether the motion description contains speeding up, slowing down, or turning. If none occurs, zero net force remains compatible.
⚡ Core1B Elicitation Contract
1. Predict: A low-friction cart is already moving when the push ends. Must a new forward force keep acting just to preserve that motion?
2. Attempt Expectation: A two-case prediction for a released moving cart and an initially resting cart under the same zero-net-force condition.
3. Reconstruct Strategy: The teaching path presents the moving and resting cases as a finished comparison. The learner route first removes the push, defines what change would look like, then discovers that both cases share unchanged velocity.
4. Boundary Test: A cart moving east begins to slow down. Can zero net force still describe that interval?
R2
Nonzero forces can cancel
MIC-PHY-NLM-FORCES-SUM-ZERO · CAP-NLM-FORCES-SUM-ZERO
Default Lane MEDIUM Pos: 45
Learner Capability Assessment: CAP-NLM-FORCES-SUM-ZERO
Error Stage: Governed Repair: NLM2-1
Primary Capability CAP-NLM-FORCES-SUM-ZERO
Capability Action Distinguish zero net force from the absence of individual forces.
Prerequisites CAP-NLM-NET-ZERO-MOTION
Intrinsic Rationale Learners often read a zero total as evidence that nothing is present instead of as cancellation among directed contributions.
🎯 What It Teaches (Inferential Jump)
Several nonzero forces can act on one body while their directed sum is zero; the motion responds to the net result rather than to the mere presence of forces.
⚖️ Controlled Variation (Rung Must Contain)
MUST CONTAIN:
  • At least two nonzero force arrows whose vector sum is zero.
  • A comparison between 'no force' and 'zero net force'.
CONTROLLED PHASES:
Phase 1: Vary the magnitudes of two opposite collinear forces together while holding their equality and opposite directions.
→ Notice: the individual arrows can grow while the net arrow remains zero
⚠️ Common Misconception & Repair
Wrong Idea: "If the net force is zero, no forces are acting."
Diagnostic: A book rests on a table. Are there no forces on the book, or can nonzero forces add to zero?
Repair: List every interaction force on the body, then combine their directions; cancellation belongs to the net result, not to the individual forces.
🏁 Exit Task & Success Criterion
An object has two equal opposite forces acting on it. Explain what is zero and what is not, then state what motion change the net result predicts.
Model Response: The net force is zero, but both individual forces are nonzero. The zero net result predicts no change of velocity.
Check: Increase both forces equally; the net remains zero even though neither individual force disappears.
⚡ Core1B Elicitation Contract
1. Predict: Two people push the same box equally hard in opposite directions. If the box has zero net force, does that mean neither person is exerting a force?
2. Attempt Expectation: A force inventory that keeps both opposite contributions visible plus a separate statement of the net result.
3. Reconstruct Strategy: The teaching path lists and combines the forces. The learner route commits to the inventory first, then derives cancellation and tests it by changing both contributions together.
4. Boundary Test: One of the two equal opposite pushes is removed. Is the net force still zero?
R3
A free-body diagram belongs to one chosen body
MIC-PHY-NLM-FBD-BODY-OWNERSHIP · CAP-NLM-FBD-BODY-OWNERSHIP
Default Lane HARD Pos: 70
Learner Capability Assessment: CAP-NLM-FBD-BODY-OWNERSHIP
Error Stage: Governed Repair: NLM3-1
Primary Capability CAP-NLM-FBD-BODY-OWNERSHIP
Capability Action Assign each force arrow to the body on which that force acts.
Prerequisites None
Intrinsic Rationale Equal-and-opposite partner forces look like natural cancellations until the learner asks which body each arrow actually acts on.
🎯 What It Teaches (Inferential Jump)
A free-body diagram contains forces acting on one chosen body only; the equal-and-opposite partner of an interaction acts on the other body and belongs on that other body's diagram.
⚖️ Controlled Variation (Rung Must Contain)
MUST CONTAIN:
  • The same interaction shown on two separate body diagrams.
  • A rule that every arrow on one diagram must name an external agent acting on that chosen body.
CONTROLLED PHASES:
Phase 1: Vary which interacting body is isolated while holding the same horse-cart interaction.
→ Notice: the equal-and-opposite partner arrow moves to the other body's diagram rather than cancelling on one diagram
Phase 2: Vary an additional external horizontal force on one body while holding the same contact between the two bodies.
→ Notice: the two bodies can have different net forces even though the two partner forces remain equal and opposite
⚠️ Common Misconception & Repair
Wrong Idea: "Equal and opposite partner forces cancel each other on one body's free-body diagram."
Diagnostic: A horse pulls a cart and the cart pulls back equally. Which of those two forces acts on the cart, and which acts on the horse?
Repair: Choose one body first. Put only forces acting on that body on its diagram; draw the partner force only when the other body is chosen.
🏁 Exit Task & Success Criterion
A person pushes a box. Describe the two contact-force partner arrows and state which one belongs on the box's free-body diagram.
Model Response: The person's force on the box belongs on the box diagram. The box's equal-and-opposite force on the person belongs on the person's diagram.
Check: Switch the chosen body to the person; the membership of the two partner arrows must switch with it.
⚡ Core1B Elicitation Contract
1. Predict: A horse pulls a cart while the cart pulls back on the horse. If you draw only the cart, should both of those horizontal forces appear on the cart's free-body diagram?
2. Attempt Expectation: Two separate body inventories, one for the cart and one for the horse, with the contact partner arrows assigned to the body each arrow acts on.
3. Reconstruct Strategy: The teaching path demonstrates two correct diagrams. The learner route begins from one chosen body and repeatedly applies the receiver test until the separation of partner arrows is forced.
4. Boundary Test: Two hands push the same box equally in opposite directions. Can those two forces both appear on the box's free-body diagram?
R5
Friction opposes relative sliding at a contact
MIC-PHY-NLM-FRICTION · CAP-NLM-FRICTION
Default Lane MEDIUM Pos: 78
Learner Capability Assessment: CAP-NLM-FRICTION
Error Stage: Governed Repair: NLM5-1
Primary Capability CAP-NLM-FRICTION
Capability Action Identify friction as a contact force that opposes relative sliding or the tendency to slide.
Prerequisites CAP-NLM-FBD-BODY-OWNERSHIP
Intrinsic Rationale Friction is often memorized as 'opposite motion', which fails for driven or rolling contacts.
🎯 What It Teaches (Inferential Jump)
Friction is a contact force along the surface that opposes relative sliding, or the tendency for relative sliding, between the contacting surfaces; it is not defined by the object's overall velocity alone.
⚖️ Controlled Variation (Rung Must Contain)
MUST CONTAIN:
  • A named contact surface and a relative-sliding or slip-tendency statement.
  • A conveyor-belt counterexample where friction need not point opposite the object's ground-frame velocity.
CONTROLLED PHASES:
Phase 1: Vary the belt speed relative to the box while holding the same contact pair.
→ Notice: friction direction follows relative slip tendency at the contact
Phase 2: Vary the object's ground-frame motion direction while holding the same relative slip tendency.
→ Notice: friction is not defined by the object's velocity alone
⚠️ Common Misconception & Repair
Wrong Idea: "Friction always points opposite the object's velocity."
Diagnostic: A moving conveyor belt speeds up a box resting on it. Can the friction on the box point in the same direction as the box's motion?
Repair: Decide the relative sliding tendency at the contact first; friction opposes that tendency.
🏁 Exit Task & Success Criterion
A box is placed on a conveyor belt moving to the right faster than the box. Before the box matches the belt speed, what direction is the friction force on the box?
Model Response: The friction force on the box points to the right.
Check: The answer is allowed to point with the box's motion because friction is defined by the contact slip tendency, not by the box's velocity alone.
⚡ Core1B Elicitation Contract
1. Predict: Must friction on a moving object always point opposite the object's motion?
2. Attempt Expectation: A contact-level slip-tendency statement and a friction arrow.
3. Reconstruct Strategy: The teaching path begins from the contact; the learner route begins from the misleading object-velocity shortcut and repairs it through relative slip.
4. Boundary Test: If there is no relative sliding and no tendency to slide at a contact, must friction be nonzero?
R6
Net force, not any one force, sets acceleration
MIC-PHY-NLM-SECOND-LAW · CAP-NLM-SECOND-LAW
Default Lane MEDIUM Pos: 86
Learner Capability Assessment: CAP-NLM-SECOND-LAW
Error Stage: Governed Repair: NLM6-1
Primary Capability CAP-NLM-SECOND-LAW
Capability Action Relate signed net external force to acceleration with F_net = m a.
Prerequisites CAP-NLM-FBD-BODY-OWNERSHIP
Intrinsic Rationale The difficult step is completing a one-body force inventory before using the simple equation.
🎯 What It Teaches (Inferential Jump)
For a chosen body of positive mass in the Grade 9 inertial-frame model, acceleration points with the signed net external force and satisfies F_net = m a.
⚖️ Controlled Variation (Rung Must Contain)
MUST CONTAIN:
  • One chosen body with all signed external forces summed before using F = ma.
  • A zero-net-force boundary that recovers zero acceleration.
CONTROLLED PHASES:
Phase 1: Vary the signed net external force while holding the same positive mass.
→ Notice: acceleration changes in direct proportion and follows the net-force sign
Phase 2: Vary the mass while holding the same net force.
→ Notice: larger mass gives smaller acceleration magnitude
⚠️ Common Misconception & Repair
Wrong Idea: "Acceleration points with whichever individual force looks largest in the diagram."
Diagnostic: Two 10 N forces act opposite each other on a body. Does either force make the body accelerate in its direction?
Repair: Add all signed external forces first; acceleration follows the net force, not an isolated arrow.
🏁 Exit Task & Success Criterion
A 4 kg body has a net external force of +12 N along a declared axis. Find its acceleration.
Model Response: The acceleration is +3 m/s^2.
Check: Multiplying 4 kg by +3 m/s^2 recovers +12 N.
⚡ Core1B Elicitation Contract
1. Predict: A body moves right while its net external force points left. Which way is its acceleration?
2. Attempt Expectation: A one-body signed force sum and a signed acceleration.
3. Reconstruct Strategy: The teaching path builds the force sum directly; the learner route starts from the body choice and uses the zero-force boundary as a final falsifier.
4. Boundary Test: If the net external force is zero on a positive-mass body, what acceleration does F_net = ma give?
R8
Quantitative static and kinetic friction conditions
MIC-PHY-NLM-FRICTION-QUANT · CAP-NLM-FRICTION-QUANT
Default Lane HARD Pos: 88
Learner Capability Assessment: CAP-NLM-FRICTION-QUANT
Error Stage: Governed Repair: NLM8-2
Primary Capability CAP-NLM-FRICTION-QUANT
Capability Action Decide whether static friction can supply the tangential force required by the chosen-body dynamics; if relative sliding is established, use the declared kinetic-friction model.
Prerequisites CAP-NLM-FRICTION, CAP-NLM-SECOND-LAW
Intrinsic Rationale The difficult decision is not substituting mu N; it is determining the required tangential force, the actual normal reaction, and whether the contact can remain static.
🎯 What It Teaches (Inferential Jump)
Static friction is a responsive contact force: solve for the friction required to prevent relative slip and compare it with the limiting value mu_s N. Equality is a threshold condition, while the kinetic model is used only after sliding is established.
⚖️ Controlled Variation (Rung Must Contain)
MUST CONTAIN:
  • A no-slip friction requirement compared against the static limit.
  • An explicit case where static friction is below its maximum.
  • A normal reaction obtained from the actual FBD rather than assumed equal to mg.
CONTROLLED PHASES:
Phase 1: Vary the applied tangential force below the slip threshold while holding the same contact, N and mu_s.
→ Notice: static friction changes to match the required no-slip value while remaining below its limit
Phase 2: Vary the contact state from impending slip to established sliding while holding the same surface pair and normal reaction.
→ Notice: the governing relation changes from the static threshold to the kinetic model
⚠️ Common Misconception & Repair
Wrong Idea: "Friction always equals mu N."
Diagnostic: A small horizontal force is applied to a block that remains at rest. Must static friction already equal mu_s N?
Repair: No. First solve the force required for no slip. Static friction matches that requirement up to its limit; equality occurs only at impending slip.
Wrong Idea: "The normal force is always mg."
Diagnostic: If an extra force presses a block into a horizontal surface, can the normal reaction still be assumed equal to mg?
Repair: Determine N from the perpendicular free-body equation. N = mg is only a special case.
🏁 Exit Task & Success Criterion
A contact has known normal reaction N and coefficient mu_s. The no-slip force equation requires tangential friction f_required. State the test for whether static contact is possible, when equality with mu_s N is justified, and what changes once sliding is established.
Model Response: Static contact is possible when |f_required| <= mu_s N. Equality |f_s| = mu_s N is justified only at impending slip. After relative sliding is established, use the kinetic model |f_k| = mu_k N with direction opposite the relative sliding.
Check: If f_required = 0, the rule must allow f_s = 0 even when mu_s N is nonzero; that falsifies the unconditional f = mu N shortcut.
⚡ Core1B Elicitation Contract
1. Predict: A block remains at rest while the applied tangential force is gradually increased. Before impending slip, must static friction already equal mu_s N?
2. Attempt Expectation: A contact-state decision that computes the no-slip friction requirement, compares it with the static limit, and selects the correct model.
3. Reconstruct Strategy: The learner begins by testing an assumed no-slip state and lets the inequality accept or reject it; the completed teaching path states the contact-state rules explicitly.
4. Boundary Test: If the no-slip equation requires zero friction while mu_s N is positive, what is the static-friction magnitude?
R9
Connected bodies and a justified common acceleration
MIC-PHY-NLM-CONNECTED-COMMON-ACCEL · CAP-NLM-CONNECTED-COMMON-ACCEL
Default Lane HARD Pos: 90
Learner Capability Assessment: CAP-NLM-CONNECTED-COMMON-ACCEL
Error Stage: Governed Repair: NLM9-1
Primary Capability CAP-NLM-CONNECTED-COMMON-ACCEL
Capability Action Identify which bodies are constrained to share one acceleration component and write consistent body-specific or whole-system Newton-II equations.
Prerequisites CAP-NLM-SECOND-LAW
Intrinsic Rationale Learners must distinguish a kinematic constraint from a force interaction and must not cancel internal forces inside individual-body equations.
🎯 What It Teaches (Inferential Jump)
Bodies share an acceleration component only when a physical constraint keeps their relative separation fixed along that coordinate; the shared acceleration can link separate Newton-II equations, while internal partner forces cancel only if the bodies are deliberately treated as one system.
⚖️ Controlled Variation (Rung Must Contain)
MUST CONTAIN:
  • A physical statement of the connection that fixes relative separation.
  • One signed Newton-II equation per body before any system-level cancellation.
  • A whole-system check that removes only internal partner forces.
CONTROLLED PHASES:
Phase 1: Vary whether the connection/contact remains active while holding the same two bodies and external forces.
→ Notice: the common-acceleration relation disappears when the constraint is lost
Phase 2: Vary whether the bodies are solved separately or as one system while holding the same physical interaction.
→ Notice: the internal force is present in body equations but cancels from the combined system sum
⚠️ Common Misconception & Repair
Wrong Idea: "Any two touching or connected bodies automatically have the same acceleration."
Diagnostic: If two blocks are touching but one loses contact, what keeps their accelerations equal after separation?
Repair: Name the active constraint first. Equal acceleration is justified only while the relative-separation condition remains enforced.
Wrong Idea: "The contact force between two bodies can be cancelled from each individual FBD because it is an internal force of the pair."
Diagnostic: On body A alone, is the force from body B external to A?
Repair: Yes. Keep it in A's equation. It cancels only after A and B are deliberately combined into one system equation.
🏁 Exit Task & Success Criterion
Two bodies are constrained to keep a fixed separation along one straight coordinate. State the acceleration relation, write the form of the two body equations, and explain when their interaction force can disappear from an equation.
Model Response: With same-sense coordinates the constraint gives a_A = a_B = a. Each body keeps the interaction force in its own Newton-II equation; the equal-and-opposite pair cancels only in a combined A+B system equation.
Check: If the connection breaks, the acceleration equality must no longer be asserted automatically.
⚡ Core1B Elicitation Contract
1. Predict: Two blocks move together without separating. Can you assign different accelerations along their common line while their separation stays fixed?
2. Attempt Expectation: A declared connection constraint plus one signed Newton-II equation per body and an optional combined-system equation.
3. Reconstruct Strategy: The learner first proves the acceleration relation from the connection and only then decides whether to keep separate body equations or add them into a system equation.
4. Boundary Test: The contact between the bodies is lost. May a_A = a_B still be imposed from the old connection?
R10
Ideal-string tension belongs to each body-specific FBD
MIC-PHY-NLM-IDEAL-STRING-TENSION · CAP-NLM-IDEAL-STRING-TENSION
Default Lane HARD Pos: 92
Learner Capability Assessment: CAP-NLM-IDEAL-STRING-TENSION
Error Stage: Governed Repair: NLM10-1
Primary Capability CAP-NLM-IDEAL-STRING-TENSION
Capability Action Use one declared ideal-string model to assign tension forces and, where justified, one common tension magnitude throughout that same string.
Prerequisites CAP-NLM-FBD-BODY-OWNERSHIP, CAP-NLM-SECOND-LAW
Intrinsic Rationale Equal tension is a model consequence, not a property of every rope/pulley setup, and the two tension arrows act on different bodies.
🎯 What It Teaches (Inferential Jump)
For one taut massless ideal string, with any redirection treated as frictionless and massless, the tension magnitude can be represented by one T throughout that same string; each body still receives its own tension force along the local string direction.
⚖️ Controlled Variation (Rung Must Contain)
MUST CONTAIN:
  • An explicit ideal-string/redirection model declaration before one common T is used.
  • Separate tension arrows on the body-specific FBDs.
  • A nonideal boundary where equal tension is refused.
CONTROLLED PHASES:
Phase 1: Vary which attached body is isolated while holding the same ideal string.
→ Notice: the tension magnitude stays T while the force arrow belongs to the selected body and follows its local string direction
Phase 2: Vary the pulley from ideal to explicitly frictional while holding the same two bodies and rope path.
→ Notice: equal tension is no longer guaranteed by the bounded model
⚠️ Common Misconception & Repair
Wrong Idea: "Tension forces at the two ends of a string cancel because they are equal and opposite."
Diagnostic: Do the two end tensions act on the same chosen body?
Repair: No. Put the tension force acting on each body in that body's own FBD. Equal magnitudes across the ideal string do not make them a cancelling pair on one body.
Wrong Idea: "Every rope over every pulley has the same tension on both sides."
Diagnostic: What model assumptions justify using one symbol T throughout the rope?
Repair: State the ideal massless string and frictionless/massless redirection assumptions first. Without them, equal tension is not automatic.
🏁 Exit Task & Success Criterion
Two bodies are attached to the same taut ideal string, redirected only by a frictionless massless fixed pulley. State the tension relation and explain where the two tension forces appear.
Model Response: Under the declared ideal model, both string segments have the same tension magnitude T. Each body has its own string-on-body tension force on its own FBD; the forces are not cancelled across separate body diagrams.
Check: If the pulley is explicitly frictional or the rope has significant mass, this capability must refuse to assume equal tension.
⚡ Core1B Elicitation Contract
1. Predict: Two masses hang from the ends of one ideal string over a fixed ideal pulley. May the same symbol T be used for the tension magnitude on both sides?
2. Attempt Expectation: An explicit model declaration plus separate body FBD tension assignments using one common magnitude only when justified.
3. Reconstruct Strategy: The learner has to earn the single tension symbol by first stating the model and assigning the two forces to their bodies.
4. Boundary Test: The pulley axle has significant friction. Does this capability still guarantee equal tension on the two sides?
R11
One fixed-pulley string gives one length constraint
MIC-PHY-NLM-SINGLE-STRING-CONSTRAINT · CAP-NLM-SINGLE-STRING-CONSTRAINT
Default Lane HARD Pos: 93
Learner Capability Assessment: CAP-NLM-SINGLE-STRING-CONSTRAINT
Error Stage: Governed Repair: NLM11-1
Primary Capability CAP-NLM-SINGLE-STRING-CONSTRAINT
Capability Action Convert constant length of one taut inextensible string over one fixed ideal pulley into the corresponding displacement, velocity, or acceleration relation.
Prerequisites CAP-NLM-CONNECTED-COMMON-ACCEL, CAP-NLM-IDEAL-STRING-TENSION
Intrinsic Rationale The learner must translate geometry into one fixed-length equation rather than memorize a pulley-count rule.
🎯 What It Teaches (Inferential Jump)
For one taut inextensible string over one fixed ideal pulley, the two changing straight segment lengths add to a constant; with coordinates measured away from the pulley this gives y_A + y_B = constant and therefore a_A + a_B = 0.
⚖️ Controlled Variation (Rung Must Contain)
MUST CONTAIN:
  • Coordinates declared along the two string segments.
  • The fixed-length equation y_A + y_B = constant.
  • The derived signed acceleration relation a_A + a_B = 0.
  • A boundary case that forces re-derivation when the pulley itself moves.
CONTROLLED PHASES:
Phase 1: Vary which end moves farther from the fixed pulley while holding the same taut inextensible string and coordinate convention.
→ Notice: the other straight segment shortens by the same amount
Phase 2: Vary the pulley from fixed to movable while holding the string remains inextensible.
→ Notice: the old two-segment length equation is no longer sufficient and must be rebuilt
⚠️ Common Misconception & Repair
Wrong Idea: "Every pulley problem has the same acceleration ratio."
Diagnostic: What part of the string length changes in this exact geometry, and is any pulley itself moving?
Repair: Write the string-length equation from the actual geometry. The equal-magnitude result here is only for one fixed pulley with two changing straight segments.
Wrong Idea: "Bodies on opposite sides of a fixed pulley have the same signed acceleration."
Diagnostic: If both coordinates are positive away from the pulley, can both segment lengths increase while total string length stays fixed?
Repair: No. Their signed changes are opposite: a_A + a_B = 0 in these coordinates.
🏁 Exit Task & Success Criterion
One taut inextensible string passes over one fixed ideal pulley, with y_A and y_B measured away from the pulley along the two hanging segments. Write the length relation and its acceleration consequence.
Model Response: The variable string length is y_A + y_B = constant, so a_A + a_B = 0. The two acceleration magnitudes are equal and their signs are opposite in the declared coordinates.
Check: If one pulley becomes movable, this two-segment relation must be rebuilt rather than reused.
⚡ Core1B Elicitation Contract
1. Predict: If mass A moves 1 cm farther from a fixed pulley on one side of a taut inextensible string, what must happen to the straight segment on the other side?
2. Attempt Expectation: A coordinate declaration, fixed-length equation, and derived velocity/acceleration relation for one fixed-pulley string.
3. Reconstruct Strategy: The learner reconstructs the acceleration relation directly from the visible changing string segments rather than being handed an acceleration ratio.
4. Boundary Test: A pulley itself is allowed to move. Can you keep using y_A + y_B = constant without re-deriving the geometry?
R7
Third-law partner forces act on different bodies
MIC-PHY-NLM-THIRD-LAW · CAP-NLM-THIRD-LAW
Default Lane MEDIUM Pos: 94
Learner Capability Assessment: CAP-NLM-THIRD-LAW
Error Stage: Governed Repair: NLM7-1
Primary Capability CAP-NLM-THIRD-LAW
Capability Action Identify Newton-third-law force pairs as equal and opposite forces of the same interaction acting on different bodies.
Prerequisites CAP-NLM-FBD-BODY-OWNERSHIP
Intrinsic Rationale Equal-and-opposite wording invites learners to cancel a pair that belongs to two different bodies.
🎯 What It Teaches (Inferential Jump)
One interaction produces two partner forces of equal magnitude and opposite direction, but they act on different bodies and therefore do not cancel in a single body's net-force sum.
⚖️ Controlled Variation (Rung Must Contain)
MUST CONTAIN:
  • The same interaction named twice as A-on-B and B-on-A.
  • Equal-and-opposite partner arrows placed on different body diagrams.
CONTROLLED PHASES:
Phase 1: Vary which body is selected for the free-body diagram while holding the same interaction pair.
→ Notice: only the force acting on the selected body appears in that diagram
Phase 2: Vary the strength of the interaction while holding the same two bodies and interaction.
→ Notice: both partner magnitudes change together while remaining opposite
⚠️ Common Misconception & Repair
Wrong Idea: "Action and reaction cancel each other, so interacting bodies cannot accelerate."
Diagnostic: When a person pushes a cart, do the person's force on the cart and the cart's force on the person both appear on the cart's free-body diagram?
Repair: No. Put each partner on the body it acts on; only then sum forces for one body.
🏁 Exit Task & Success Criterion
A swimmer pushes water backward. Identify the third-law partner force and state which body each force acts on.
Model Response: The swimmer pushes the water backward, and the water pushes the swimmer forward with an equal-magnitude opposite force. The two forces act on different bodies.
Check: Only the water-on-swimmer force belongs on the swimmer's free-body diagram.
⚡ Core1B Elicitation Contract
1. Predict: If action and reaction are equal and opposite, why do they not automatically make the net force on one object zero?
2. Attempt Expectation: Two agent-target force statements and one arrow on each body.
3. Reconstruct Strategy: The teaching path names agent-target pairs before drawing; the learner route starts from the cancellation paradox and resolves it by body ownership.
4. Boundary Test: Can a third-law partner force ever be the force that balances another force on the same body?
R4
An accelerating observer needs a declared pseudo-force convention
MIC-PHY-NLM-FRAME-CHOICE · CAP-NLM-FRAME-CHOICE
Explicit Branch HARD Pos: 100
Learner Capability Assessment: CAP-NLM-FRAME-CHOICE
Error Stage: Governed Repair: NLM4-1
Primary Capability CAP-NLM-FRAME-CHOICE
Capability Action Decide whether an observer description requires a pseudo-force.
Prerequisites CAP-NLM-FBD-BODY-OWNERSHIP
Intrinsic Rationale The same physical interactions can be described by observers with different accelerations, and the extra convention is easy to omit when the observer is inside the accelerating vehicle.
🎯 What It Teaches (Inferential Jump)
A force-based account depends on the observer: a non-accelerating observer uses the physical interaction forces, while an accelerating observer must add the corresponding pseudo-force convention to describe the same observed motion consistently.
⚖️ Controlled Variation (Rung Must Contain)
MUST CONTAIN:
  • Two observers describing the same object, one in a non-accelerating frame and one in an accelerating frame.
  • An explicit declaration of the chosen frame before the force sum is written.
CONTROLLED PHASES:
Phase 1: Vary the observer from roadside to steadily moving bus while holding the physical interactions and the bus's zero acceleration.
→ Notice: no extra force is needed when the frames differ only by constant velocity
Phase 2: Vary the bus from steady speed to forward acceleration while holding the same object and physical interactions.
→ Notice: the bus-frame account now needs an added backward pseudo-force
⚠️ Common Misconception & Repair
Wrong Idea: "The same ordinary-force diagram can be used unchanged in any accelerating observer's frame."
Diagnostic: A loose mass appears to accelerate backward inside a bus that starts forward. Can the bus observer use only the same physical-force inventory as the roadside observer?
Repair: State the observer first. Use physical interactions alone for a non-accelerating observer; for an accelerating observer include the corresponding pseudo-force consistently.
🏁 Exit Task & Success Criterion
A bus accelerates forward and a loose object appears to move backward relative to the bus. Compare the roadside and bus-observer force descriptions without calculating any magnitude.
Model Response: The roadside description uses the physical interactions only. The accelerating bus description adds a backward pseudo-force convention to account for the observer's acceleration.
Check: Repeat the comparison once the bus moves at steady speed; the extra pseudo-force should no longer be needed for the observer change alone.
⚡ Core1B Elicitation Contract
1. Predict: A bus starts forward and a loose object seems to shift backward to a passenger. Before adding any new force, did a new physical agent suddenly begin pulling the object backward?
2. Attempt Expectation: Two labelled observer descriptions of the same object: roadside and accelerating bus, with a statement of where the pseudo-force convention appears.
3. Reconstruct Strategy: The teaching path compares two observer accounts directly. The learner route first rules out a new physical agent, identifies which observer accelerates, then introduces the pseudo-force as a modelling convention.
4. Boundary Test: The bus now travels at constant velocity. Does changing from the roadside observer to the bus observer by itself require the added pseudo-force?

1. Q-PHY-NLM-2A-COV-05 General NLM
A 4 kg body has a net external force of +12 N along a declared axis. Find its acceleration.
Solution / Key: The acceleration is +3 m/s^2.
2. Q-PHY-NLM-2A-01 General NLM
One cart is at rest and another moves steadily in a straight line. For each, decide whether zero net force is compatible with the motion and explain the common rule.
Solution / Key: Zero net force is compatible with both. Each cart can keep its current velocity unchanged.
3. Q-PHY-NLM-2A-COV-02 General NLM
An object has two equal opposite forces acting on it. Explain what is zero and what is not, then state what motion change the net result predicts.
Solution / Key: The net force is zero, but both individual forces are nonzero. The zero net result predicts no change of velocity.
4. Q-PHY-NLM-2A-COV-03 General NLM
A person pushes a box. Describe the two contact-force partner arrows and state which one belongs on the box's free-body diagram.
Solution / Key: The person's force on the box belongs on the box diagram. The box's equal-and-opposite force on the person belongs on the person's diagram.
5. Q-PHY-NLM-2A-COV-04 General NLM
A box is placed on a conveyor belt moving to the right faster than the box. Before the box matches the belt speed, what direction is the friction force on the box?
Solution / Key: The friction force on the box points to the right.
6. Q-PHY-NLM-2A-COV-06 General NLM
A swimmer pushes water backward. Identify the third-law partner force and state which body each force acts on.
Solution / Key: The swimmer pushes the water backward, and the water pushes the swimmer forward with an equal-magnitude opposite force. The two forces act on different bodies.
7. Q-PHY-NLM-PRACTICAL-12 General NLM
Plan a trolley–pulley–hanging-mass experiment to verify Newton’s second law. Describe one controlled variation that tests the effect of net force at fixed system mass, what you measure, and what result would support F_net = m a.
Solution / Key: Keep the total moving mass approximately fixed while transferring masses between trolley and hanger so the driving force changes, measure acceleration for each trial, and test whether acceleration changes in direct proportion to the corrected net driving force.
8. Q-PHY-NLM-2A-FRICTION-THRESHOLD-01 General NLM
A small block of mass m rests on a larger block of mass M. The floor under the larger block is smooth. A horizontal force F is applied to the larger block. The coefficient of static friction between the blocks is mu_s. Derive the largest value of F for which the two blocks can move together without slipping.
Solution / Key: While there is no slip, both blocks have a = F/(M+m). The upper block needs friction f_required = m a. Since N = m g here, no slip requires mF/(M+m) <= mu_s m g, so F <= mu_s(M+m)g. Therefore F_max = mu_s(M+m)g.
9. Q-PHY-NLM-2A-CONNECTED-02 General NLM
Two blocks A and B of masses m_A and m_B remain in contact on a smooth horizontal floor. A horizontal force F pushes A toward B, and the contact is maintained. Derive their common acceleration and the magnitude of the contact force exerted by A on B.
Solution / Key: The fixed contact keeps the blocks at one common acceleration a = F/(m_A+m_B). For block B alone, the contact force is its only horizontal force, so N_AB = m_B a = m_B F/(m_A+m_B).
10. Q-PHY-NLM-2A-IDEAL-STRING-03 General NLM
Two carts A and B of masses m_A and m_B are connected by one taut massless inextensible string on a smooth horizontal track. A horizontal force F pulls cart A away from B along the string. Derive the common acceleration and the string tension. Then name one model change that would make equal tension throughout the connector no longer guaranteed.
Solution / Key: The string constraint gives one acceleration a = F/(m_A+m_B). For B, T = m_B a, so T = m_B F/(m_A+m_B). Equal tension relies on the ideal massless-string model; a connector with significant mass or a nonideal redirection can invalidate one common T.
11. Q-PHY-NLM-2A-FIXED-PULLEY-04 General NLM
One taut inextensible string passes over one fixed pulley. Let y_A and y_B be the two straight segment lengths measured positive away from the pulley. Write the string-length relation and derive the corresponding velocity and acceleration relations. If A accelerates away from the pulley with magnitude a, state B's signed acceleration in these coordinates.
Solution / Key: The variable string length obeys y_A + y_B = constant. Therefore v_A + v_B = 0 and a_A + a_B = 0. If a_A = +a away from the pulley, then a_B = -a, toward the pulley.
12. Q-PHY-NLM-2A-FRAME-CHOICE-07 General NLM
A bus accelerates to the right on a straight road. A loose object on a smooth horizontal shelf is described first by a roadside observer and then by a passenger in the bus. Explain how the two descriptions differ and where, if anywhere, a pseudo-force is used.
Solution / Key: The roadside description uses only physical interactions; the loose object does not acquire a new backward physical force merely because the bus accelerates. In the accelerating bus frame, a backward pseudo-force may be introduced as a modelling convention so Newton's laws can be written in that non-inertial frame.

Core 2B Transfer Items (5 Questions)

1. Q-PHY-NLM-2B-FRICTION-STATE-01 Core 2B Transfer
A crate rests on a rough horizontal floor. It is pulled by a force P at an upward angle theta. The coefficients mu_s and mu_k are known, but the statement does not say whether the crate slips. For a specified P, determine the crate's acceleration or justify that it remains at rest.
Transfer Demand: First find N = mg - P sin(theta). Under a provisional no-slip state, the friction required is f_required = P cos(theta). If |f_required| <= mu_s N, the crate can remain at rest and static friction equals the required value. If that inequality fails, sliding occurs; then use kinetic friction mu_k N opposite the sliding and apply Newton II to find acceleration.
2. Q-PHY-NLM-2B-CONNECTED-SYSTEM-02 Core 2B Transfer
Three identical carts A, B and C move in a line on a smooth track. A pushes B, B pushes C, and contact is maintained while an external force F is applied only to A. Without being told which system to choose, find the acceleration of the three-cart train and the contact force that B exerts on C.
Transfer Demand: Use A+B+C as one system for the acceleration: a = F/(3m). Then isolate C to find the requested contact force: N_BC = m a = F/3. The B-on-C force is internal only to the three-cart system; it is external to cart C and must remain in C's equation.
3. Q-PHY-NLM-2B-STRING-MODEL-03 Core 2B Transfer
Two masses are connected by one rope over a fixed pulley. The pulley axle is explicitly stated to have appreciable friction. A proposed solution writes the same tension T on both sides of the pulley before doing any calculation. Decide whether that step is justified by the Grade9V3 ideal-string model and explain what can still be said without introducing a new pulley model.
Transfer Demand: The one-common-T step is not justified by the bounded ideal-string/redirection model because a required ideal assumption has been broken. Each mass still has a rope tension force on its own free-body diagram, but this capability must not assert equal magnitudes across the nonideal pulley. A quantitative solution would require an additional model that is outside this slice.
4. Q-PHY-NLM-2B-PULLEY-REPRESENTATION-04 Core 2B Transfer
A diagram is replaced by this verbal description: one taut inextensible string passes over one fixed pulley, with mass A on the left end and mass B on the right end. Coordinates x_A and x_B are both defined positive upward. Before writing any Newton-II equations, construct the string-length relation and derive the signed acceleration relation in these coordinates.
Transfer Demand: Because the changing segment lengths decrease when x_A or x_B increases, the variable string length can be written (C_A - x_A) + (C_B - x_B) = constant. Hence x_A + x_B = constant, so v_A + v_B = 0 and a_A + a_B = 0. Thus if A accelerates upward, B accelerates downward with equal magnitude.
5. Q-PHY-NLM-2B-FRAME-SELECTION-05 Core 2B Transfer
A small bob hangs from the roof of a car that accelerates horizontally to the right. A student wants the bob to be at rest in the chosen coordinates while finding the string direction. Decide which observer description makes that possible, state the extra modelling term required there, and give an independent inertial-frame alternative without solving for a numerical angle.
Transfer Demand: To keep the bob at rest in the chosen coordinates, use the accelerating car frame and include a pseudo-force m a_car to the left in addition to the physical forces. Alternatively, use the roadside inertial frame with only physical forces and give the bob the same rightward acceleration as the car. Both descriptions must predict the same string direction.

🛡️ Deliberate Curriculum & Model Boundaries (Strictly Out of Scope)

To prevent scope creep and preserve pedagogical fidelity, the following mechanics domains are explicitly declared out-of-scope for the NLM First Law matrix slice:

  • No broad collision-conservation course: Isolated to dedicated momentum and impulse packages.
  • No rocket equation: Variable-mass systems are strictly excluded from basic second-law analysis.
  • No general compound / movable pulley systems: Bound strictly to 1 fixed pulley and 1 continuous string.
  • No full non-inertial mechanics course: Bounded to 1D linear accelerating frame choice with declared pseudo-force.
  • No rotational dynamics: Torque and moment of inertia remain in rigid-body rotation matrices.
