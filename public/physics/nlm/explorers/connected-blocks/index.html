⚡ Governing Invariants & Connected Multi-Body Pedagogy:
1.
The Contact Force Fallacy (N₁₂ ≠ F): When pushing a train of touching blocks with external force F, the normal contact force at the interface does not equal F! N₁₂ = [m₂ / (m₁ + m₂)] × F < F. Block 1 uses part of F to accelerate itself (m₁ a); only the remaining fraction is transmitted to push Block 2!
2.
Newton's Third Law & Body Ownership (R3 & R7): The contact pair consists of equal and opposite forces: N⃗₁→₂ = -N⃗₂→₁. These forces act on different bodies! They NEVER appear on the same isolated free-body diagram and therefore never cancel on one body.
3.
Push Direction Asymmetry Invariant: Reversing the direction of push preserves system acceleration: a = F / (m₁ + m₂), but drastically changes the internal contact force! Pushing from left gives N₁₂ = [m₂ / (m₁ + m₂)] F, while pushing from right gives N'₁₂ = [m₁ / (m₁ + m₂)] F.

🔄 The Push Reversal Paradox

Pushing from Left ($F$ on $m_1$): $N_{12} = \frac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2} F$.
Pushing from Right ($F$ on $m_2$): $N'_{12} = \frac{m_1}{m_1 + m_2} F$.

• If $m_1 = 2\text{ kg}$ and $m_2 = 8\text{ kg}$, with $F = 50\text{ N}$:
  Pushing left gives $N_{12} = \frac{8}{10} \times 50 = \mathbf{40\text{ N}}$.
  Pushing right gives $N'_{12} = \frac{2}{10} \times 50 = \mathbf{10\text{ N}}$!
• The acceleration is identical ($5\text{ m/s}^2$), but the contact stress drops by 75%!

⚖️ Why Action & Reaction Never Cancel

$N_{1\to 2}$ acts on Block 2 to push it forward. $N_{2\to 1}$ acts on Block 1 resisting the push. Because they act on different bodies, neither block experiences zero net force! They cancel only when you draw a system boundary around BOTH blocks together.

🧠 Targeted Diagnostic Challenges Requires Explorer Invariants

These challenges diagnose and eliminate common contact-force and Third Law fallacies.

Question 1 · The Full-Force Contact Fallacy Diagnostic 3.1
Two blocks with masses m₁ = 3 kg and m₂ = 2 kg rest on a frictionless horizontal surface in contact. A horizontal push of F = 20 N is applied to m₁ pushing towards m₂. What is the magnitude of the contact normal force between the blocks?
Explorer Explanation: Option B is correct! First find the common acceleration: a = F / (m₁ + m₂) = 20 / 5 = 4 m/s². The only horizontal force acting on Block 2 is the contact normal force N₁₂, so by Newton's Second Law on Block 2: N₁₂ = m₂ a = 2 × 4 = 8 N. Block 1 consumes 12 N (m₁ a) to accelerate itself, transmitting only the remaining 8 N to Block 2!
Question 2 · The Push Reversal Symmetry Trap Diagnostic 3.2
In the same setup (m₁ = 3 kg, m₂ = 2 kg), the same 20 N force is now applied to m₂ pushing toward m₁ (from right to left). How does the new contact force N'₁₂ compare to the previous contact force N₁₂?
Explorer Explanation: Option B is correct! When pushing m₂, the contact force must accelerate m₁ in front of it. Since m₁ = 3 kg is heavier than m₂ = 2 kg, a greater contact force is required to impart the same 4 m/s² acceleration: N'₁₂ = m₁ a = 3 × 4 = 12 N! Reversing the push direction changes the contact force from 8 N to 12 N!
Question 3 · The Third Law Action-Reaction Cancellation Myth Diagnostic 3.3
A student argues: "According to Newton's Third Law, if Block 1 exerts a force of 8 N on Block 2, then Block 2 exerts an equal and opposite force of 8 N on Block 1. Since these two forces are equal and opposite (8 N - 8 N = 0), the blocks can never accelerate!" What is the fundamental flaw in this reasoning?
Explorer Explanation: Option B is the foundational insight of Newton's Third Law (R3 & R7)! To determine if Block 1 accelerates, you sum ONLY the forces acting ON Block 1: F_net,1 = F - N₂₁ = 20 - 8 = 12 N ≠ 0! The force N₁₂ acts on Block 2, so it cannot cancel forces acting on Block 1. Action-reaction pairs only cancel when you take the combined system of both bodies together.
