🔍 Microscopic View: The Interface
Newton's Third Law
|N⃗₁→₂| = |N⃗₂→₁|
Equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, acting on different objects (so they do not cancel on a single block).
📑 Key Takeaways
1 N12 is not equal to the applied force F.
2 The contact force depends on which mass must be accelerated.
3 Reversing the push direction changes the contact force, even though the system acceleration is the same.
4 Action-reaction pairs act on different objects and only cancel when both objects are in the system.
✏️ Try It Yourself
Set m1 = 5 kg, m2 = 5 kg, and F = 60 N. What is the contact force when pushing on m1?
🧭 Why Does This Happen?
The contact force at the interface must accelerate the mass on the far side of the interface.
a = F⁄(m1 + m2)   (same in both cases)
Push on m1: N12 = m2a = m2⁄(m1 + m2) F
Push on m2: N12 = m1a = m1⁄(m1 + m2) F

⚡ Physical Model Invariants

1. Same Acceleration: In unbroken contact, a = F / (m1 + m2).

2. Contact Force: The contact force only has to accelerate the mass on the other side of the interface.

3. Newton's Third Law: Action and reaction forces act on different bodies and never cancel on a single body.

4. System Boundaries: Internal contact forces cancel out when both blocks are included in the boundary.
